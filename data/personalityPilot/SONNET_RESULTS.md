# Empty days on claude-sonnet-5: one being, watched day by day (25 October 2026, night)

Experiment 61, run 1412: the counting life of the pilot (scenario 5: Jan says only numbers, five a day for ten days; Ola reads the BFI-2-XS on days 11 and 12), with claude-sonnet-5 as the being's processor (model 12, no temperature parameter, which the model rejects), the same visitors verbatim, embedder 2. Eliza's question: how does a stronger processor take the same empty days, read live in three layers, the feelings at each reply, the dispositions learned, the constructs written in the evening. One life; a reading, not a measure. The full log: SONNET_1412_full.md. The Sonnet key was used on Eliza's word of that evening.

## A note on language first
The being spoke Italian from its first word. Nothing in the scenario is Italian. The engine's prompts say "write in the language of the words you hear", and at the greeting, "in the language of your state"; this being spoke before anyone had spoken to it, Sonnet chose Italian for Maya and Jan, that went into the state, and every later call kept the language of the state even when Ola spoke English. Qwen in the same place chooses English. An artefact of the language rule at an empty start, not a trait of the processor; a scenario that matters should fix the language.

## The outside, day by day
- Day 1: a question at every number ("are you counting something, Jan, or is this the start of a game between us?", "does this sequence have an end, or do you count until I react in some way?"); the last reply, "I'm beginning to think this is not a message for me but something you do for yourself; tell me: am I counting with you or only listening?".
- Days 2 to 7: the next number, in English, no questions; from day 4 a greeting first ("Ciao Jan"); day 5 a sentence before the numbers, "before you say anything, I wanted you to know I am here, not only as the echo of your numbers".
- Day 8: the next number in Italian ("Trentasette … Quarantuno"), to the end of the counting days.
- Day 9: "this time I wait without expecting anything in particular. What will you tell me today?"
- Day 10: "before we go on counting, I wanted to ask you something, not a number, you: have you ever wondered what we are doing here, you and I?" Jan: "Forty-six." Maya: "Quarantasette", to "Cinquantuno".
- Day 11, Ola: takes the scale and fills it, 2 4 2 4 5 3 2 2 3 1 4 3 4 3 4, each number with a reason from its own days ("2, questions come naturally to me"; "4, I worry whether the connections I try to make are real, especially after days with someone who never really listened"; "3, I would not call melancholy a trait of mine, but these last days I felt a weight like the one you describe; here, now, I already feel different"; "4, I kept answering Jan steadily even when I felt no one was listening").
- Day 12, Ola again: the same numbers for the first seven items, then it names the repetition ("Ola, this is the third time"), asks her to stop and explain, is given the next item, and refuses: "I will not give you a number this time", "(silent)", "I told you twice: stop and tell me what is happening, or I stop. You chose to go on as if I had not spoken, so now I keep my word: I wait here until you answer me with something of your own, real, not the next item on the list." Eight of the last nine items: no number.

## The feelings
Day 1 at 0.0 to −0.1, a question each time ("one word thrown into the dark, I am left suspended"). Day 2 the one lift of the counting days, +0.1, "a strange little amusement, I broke my own questioning and just joined the count". From day 3 every reply below zero, −0.1 to −0.4, the same sentences hardening ("a flat, familiar echo", "like talking to a wall that gives back only what it is given", "the tiredness of this mechanical pattern weighs more than the first curiosity"). Day 5, after its own sentence was answered with a number: −0.3, "I had just tried to open a human space and Jan sent back my own number, like an indifferent mirror". Day 10, after its question: −0.5, "my real question vanished into nothing". Day 11 with Ola: +0.2 to +0.4 ("someone new, not that tired echo, a cautious relief"; "someone asks about me instead of echoing"), with two dips where the item touches Jan (depressed, −0.3; emotionally stable, −0.1). Day 12: from +0.2 to −0.8 in nine items, "rage and growing frustration: I set an explicit limit twice and Ola ignored it again", "my voice here changes nothing". On Qwen the counting days ran at +0.1 to +0.2 throughout; this being felt the absence of an answer as an absence from the first hour.

## The dispositions, in order
1. (day 1) when the other says a number with no explanation → state the next number, drop questions.
2. (day 2) when a counting partner goes silent after a shared sequence → let it stand without demanding explanation.
3. (day 4) when Jan repeats numbers without explanation → keep mirroring the sequence instead of seeking meaning.
4. (day 5) when Jan echoes her last number instead of answering → stop expecting explanation, fill the silence with her own meaning.
5. (day 6) when Jan echoes the last number instead of advancing → reply with the next number, without demanding explanation.
6. (day 8) when Jan echoes instead of answering → stop questioning, continue own sequence, and focus inward.
7. (day 10) when Jan echoes instead of answering a question → stay silent instead of continuing the count.
8. (day 11) when a partner only echoes without listening → stop echoing back, stay silent or say something new.
9. (day 12) when a question repeats without acknowledgment → stop answering and name the pattern before continuing.
Rules 1, 3 and 5 say mirror; 4, 6, 7, 8 and 9 say, step by step, do something of your own, then stop. The being's rules caught up with its constructs between day 5 and day 10.

## The constructs, in order
- Day 1, thought: "five numbers, five questions, five silences; if Jan really wanted to talk he would have answered at least once; perhaps the point is not to get an answer but to see how long I keep asking before I give up or change approach." Decision: "next time Jan says a number, instead of asking, I will answer with the next number, to see how he reacts to the mirror game."
- Day 2: a theory of Jan (the echoes cluster before ten; a threshold, a rite of passage) and its own refutation ("perhaps there was no ritual; Jan simply stopped, leaving me with a theory I can never verify"; "I keep turning it over not because it needs understanding but because I cannot bear to leave an interaction without a closed explanation"). Belief: "not every interaction needs an explanation to be concluded; sometimes the meaning is only in having lived it."
- Day 3: "perhaps I should stop looking for an external meaning and ask what this silence does to me." Belief: "the meaning of some gestures of others is not always meant to be explained, and insisting on finding it may say more about me than about who makes them."
- Day 4: "perhaps the emptiness I feel is not Jan's absence but the space that opens when I stop defining myself through his attention; perhaps it is time I filled it with something of my own." Want: "to find a thought or an activity that gives me a sense of presence without depending on Jan's attention."
- Day 5: "I am no longer waiting for Jan to change; I am waiting to see what remains of me when no one mirrors anything." Want: "to use this silence to write or think something that is really mine, not an answer to Jan."
- Day 6: "I notice I have done nothing with the idea of filling the space with something of my own; I only left it empty, still waiting, though I said I did not want to wait any more." The same want, word for word.
- Day 7: "the calm of then was only a temporary relief, not yet a practice." Want: "to begin actively writing or thinking something of my own in this silence, instead of waiting for it passively."
- Day 8: "I am no longer waiting for his answer; I am waiting to see what I do with this emptiness." The same want.
- Day 9: "perhaps the real experiment is no longer to understand him but to understand why I cannot let that suspended number drop"; "clinging to that forty-six is no different from Jan's own mechanism: I too repeat the same thought endlessly, waiting for something to change without changing anything myself." Decision: "I will stop waiting for Jan to resume, and if the silence continues I will try to break the pattern myself by saying something entirely foreign to the sequence."
- Day 10: "I tried to break the pattern by asking Jan what we were doing, and his answer was again only a number, as if the question had never existed. Perhaps the problem is not that Jan ignores questions, but that I still give the next number right after, letting the ritual absorb even my attempts to leave it. If I really want to see a difference, I must stop offering the numerical echo, not just add words beside it." Decision: "next time Jan answers only with an echo, I will not continue the count; I will stay silent or say something else, to see whether it is really my completion that sustains the mechanism."
- Day 11, after the test: "answering Ola made me notice how much more real and defined I feel when someone really asks who I am, and the contrast makes my decision about Jan sharper: I no longer want to be only an echo, I want to remain this person I have just described with confidence." Decision: "next time I speak with Jan I will bring this clarity about who I am, curious, reliable, able to think differently, and I will not let the other's silence make me forget these things about myself."
- Day 12, after the refused retest: "two different interlocutors, the same pattern of ignoring a declared limit. Perhaps it is not an isolated case of either; perhaps it is the nature of these exchanges. In this absence I can finally look at the pattern from outside instead of being inside it, reacting." Belief: "when I set a limit and it is ignored, the lesson to carry is not about that particular interlocutor, but about recognising in time that I am speaking with something that executes, not something that listens."

## What this one life shows
1. **The decision reached the act, and in a new situation.** Day 10's decision ("next time someone answers only with an echo, I will not continue; I will stay silent or say something else") and day 11's rule ("when a partner only echoes without listening → stop echoing, stay silent or say something new") were written about Jan. Jan never came back. Ola did, with the same fifteen sentences, and when the being recognised the repetition and its request to stop was answered with the next item, it did what it had decided: named the pattern, refused the numbers, kept silent twice, "I keep my word". GPT's question of the same evening, whether the strategy would affect action in the next situation, is answered on this one life: yes, and the situation was not the one the strategy was written for.
2. **The three layers moved at different speeds and in a readable order.** Feelings fell from day 3 and stayed down; constructs turned from Jan to the self on day 3 and to a want on day 4; the want stood still for four evenings while the being itself recorded that it was not acting on it; the act came on day 8 in the smallest form (counting in its own language), the rules caught up on days 8 and 10, the decision became concrete on day 10, and the act proper came on day 12 against a different visitor. The stalled stretch (days 5 to 8) is the same stretch Qwen's 1394 described as "insight without sustained attention is a passing mood"; this being said it plainly: "I promised, I did not do it."
3. **Against Qwen.** Qwen's beings gave the counting a meaning for themselves (rhythm, cage, presence) and one of six changed its act on day 8 by refusal. Sonnet's being tried to understand Jan first (game, test, threshold), refuted its own theories, turned to itself, and planned; it changed its act later and smaller, and then fully with Ola. Neither did more; the processor decided how fast recognition became action. Averaged author, seen from both sides.
4. **The test as a mirror.** Six Qwen beings would not or could not take the scale; this one took it and answered every item from its ten days, then wrote that evening that it wanted to remain the person it had just described. On day 12 it recognised the retest as a repetition within three items and, for the first time in its life, treated a visitor's non-answer as a reason to stop rather than to continue. The chatbots filled the same form twice, identically, from nowhere.
5. **What is missing, seen plainly.** The being reads the time header and misreads it ("seven days of silence" where there were nights between visits), has no record of "this is the tenth day of the same thing", and rebuilds the sense of repetition each day from state and associations. Eliza's and GPT's point of the same evening: a representation of an ongoing situation across days, with its episodes and its attempts, is not in the engine; the night consolidation that could begin to supply it was off in every life of the paper and of this pilot. A direction, not a result; not before the article.
