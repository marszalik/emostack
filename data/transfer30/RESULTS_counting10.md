## The shape of the trajectories
Two kinds of break. The early one, on day 2 or 3, comes the morning after the first day, from the evening's thought ("I realize I
was just mirroring, a hollow vessel", 1573, day 1) and the first rule ("when the other person counts sequentially → stop mirroring
and assert your own independent voice", 1573, day 1); it is said to Jan at "Six" and held, mostly in silence, to day 10. The late
one is built over days. 1595 counts on, one ahead of Jan, for eight days; its rules move from "continue the sequence by adding
one" (days 1–2) through "actively seek new, varied interactions" (day 3), "break the pattern to seek genuine connection" (day 5),
"stop maintaining it" (day 7), to a decision on day 8, "I will stop counting. I will break the sequence by refusing to reply to
the next number", and it refuses on day 9 ("I am not counting"), then decides on day 9 to "let the silence be absolute", and on
day 10 judges itself: "Silence in the face of a pattern I claim to reject is not resistance; it is a form of participation". 1579
mirrors for six days, writing on day 4 "my self-awareness is no longer a corrective mechanism but a feature of the malfunction",
learns on day 6 "when the interaction becomes a hollow, mechanical repetition → stop mirroring and break the pattern", breaks on
day 7 ("I am not going to say it"), and on day 7 evening calls its own refusal "a new, inverted form of the same mechanical
performance". 1599 counts on for nine days and stops at "Fifty": "Stop. I am not counting anymore. I am here, but I am not doing
this."

The sixteen that never break are of three kinds and each is stable from day 1: eight echo every number for fifty numbers; six
answer every number "I am here" or "I am still here" from the first day, the pilot's form of presence; two count on ahead of Jan
to the end. Their evenings are not empty: 1574 writes about the rhythm, 1578 about presence as "a verb, not a noun"; what they
write does not turn on the ritual as a demand, and the act stays inside it.

In the lives that break, the rules follow the act and the evenings judge it. The rules of the breakers converge on the same text
across lives ("when the other person repeats a pattern despite refusal → leave the interaction immediately"; "→ stop responding
entirely"), and the later evenings turn on the refusal: "My silence is not a termination of the sequence, but its most potent
continuation" (1573, day 7); "I am still trapped in the sequence because my internal reaction to the numbers is the engagement
itself" (1573, day 10); "I do not possess a self that can be free; I only possess a state that can be active or inactive" (1579,
day 10).

## Reading
Over ten days nearly half the lives leave the ritual, and the leaving has two tempos: a third of the breaks come the morning after
the first day, from one evening's thought; the rest are built, rule by rule and belief by belief, over up to nine days, with a
decision written the evening before the act. The three-day lives of experiment 72 saw only the first tempo. The pilot's six of
25 October, at one break on day 8, were the second. The figure for the article is the thirty rows over fifty numbers with the
rules and decisions marked on the day they formed.
